## Post-Mortem Study – HubSpot CRM Migration Risk Prompt

### 1. Prompt Objective

The prompt was designed to identify **3 realistic implementation risks** for a HubSpot CRM migration and assess each risk based on **probability, impact, and mitigation**.

### 2. What Worked Well

- Focused specifically on **CRM migration risks**.
- Used a simple and structured table format.
- Included realistic risks: **data migration, user adoption, and system integration**.
- Used consistent **Low / Medium / High** ratings.
- Provided practical mitigation actions for each risk.
- Added an overall risk-area summary with priorities.

### 3. Key Findings

| Area                | Finding                                         | Lesson Learned                                            |
| ------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Risk Identification | Risks were relevant to CRM migration            | Focus on technical, operational, and people-related risks |
| Risk Assessment     | Probability and impact were clearly categorized | Use consistent rating definitions                         |
| Mitigation          | Actions were practical and actionable           | Each risk should have a specific response                 |
| Presentation        | Tables made the output easy to review           | Use structured formats for management reporting           |

### 4. Improvement Opportunities

- Define what **Low, Medium, and High** mean before assigning ratings.
- Include **risk owners** and target dates for a more complete risk register.
- Add **early warning indicators** to identify risks before they become major issues.
- Consider additional risks such as **security/privacy, scope creep, and CRM configuration errors**.

### 5. Overall Conclusion

The prompt successfully produced a concise CRM migration risk assessment. For a real project, the analysis could be strengthened by adding **risk owners, early warning indicators, contingency plans, and measurable mitigation actions**.
